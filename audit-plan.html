The plan

Check what we can trust.

I'll look at the question bank and student activity, then turn the findings into a short list of things to keep, fix, rewrite or remove.

How I'll audit

The aim is to understand what the data is telling us before we change the product.

01

Check the records

Look for missing values, duplicates, broken links between tables, invalid scores and questions that do not render correctly.

02

Look for patterns

Compare accuracy, completion, response time and second attempts. A single number is rarely enough to explain what happened.

03

Decide what to do

Write down the evidence, the likely cause, the owner and the next action. Keep the original data unchanged.

Question bank

I want to know whether a low score reflects learning difficulty or a problem with the question itself.

Almost everyone gets it wrong

The answer key or the wording may be wrong.

Action: Check the key, explanation and question.

Strong students fail, weak students pass

The question may be confusing or testing the wrong skill.

Action: Review the options and rewrite or retire it.

One wrong option attracts most answers

Two options may look correct, or the intended answer may be unclear.

Action: Fix the key or make the option clearer.

Some options are never selected

The question may be easier to guess than we think.

Action: Replace the options that are not useful.

The difficulty label does not match results

A question marked hard may be easy in practice, or the other way round.

Action: Recheck the label using real results.

Students skip, time out or answer unusually fast

The question may be unclear, too long or being guessed.

Action: Review the wording, interaction and time limit.

The same question appears more than once

Repeats split the results and give students the same task again.

Action: Merge the records or remove one version.

The question is broken

Missing answers, blank options or unloaded images make the result unusable.

Action: Repair it or stop serving it.

Student activity

This tells us where students need help and whether the learning loop is helping them improve.

Where do students spend time but score poorly?

These topics may need more teaching time or better practice.

Action: Prioritise the weakest objectives.

Where does performance fall at higher thinking levels?

A student may remember an idea but struggle to use it.

Action: Adjust the question mix.

Which activities do students finish?

If students finish tests but abandon homework, the homework may be too long.

Action: Reduce the size and compare completion again.

Where do students stop in a test?

A common drop-off point may mean the test is too long.

Action: Check the drop-off before changing difficulty.

Who is probably guessing?

Very fast wrong answers or the same option repeatedly can distort mastery.

Action: Mark those attempts separately.

Do second attempts improve?

This is a direct check of whether the learning loop is working.

Action: Compare the same topic across attempts.

Rules I'll follow

These keep the audit from turning a small or messy sample into a big conclusion.

A1

Use enough attempts

I won't call a question broken when only a few students have seen it. Small samples go on a watch list.

A2

Compare similar groups

I'll separate grade, subject, region, test type and time period when those differences affect the result.

A3

Keep incomplete attempts separate

A skip, timeout and wrong answer are different events. I won't count them as the same thing.

A4

Do not overwrite source data

The audit will produce a separate report. Any fix will happen in a controlled staging environment.

What I'll deliver

A short problem list that the team can review and assign.

Each finding will include

The affected records or group, the evidence, the likely reason, how confident we are, and who should decide the next step.
